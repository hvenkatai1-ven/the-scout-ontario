The competition, and what to build next

The Scout · Ontario
Landscape review
August 2026
Three categories · nine apps · seven decisions

Nothing on the market does exactly what The Scout does, but the job is split cleanly across three categories, and each one is mature. The short answer: the light planners own precision, the spot maps own discovery, and a new crop of save-from-a-reel apps owns the social layer. None of them owns the day.

PhotoPills is the category leader and also its cautionary tale. Photographers describe it as having the most features and the steepest curve — several keep The Photographer's Ephemeris open beside it because it opens straight onto a map instead of a menu.

The opening for The Scout is the join: a trending spot is worthless if the light is wrong, the lot is full, or the reservation sold out. Everything recommended below serves that join, and nothing else.

PhotoPills
iOS · Android · one-time purchase

The reference implementation: a map planner that answers either "good location at this time" or "best date and time at this location", plus AR sun, moon and Milky Way paths and a wall of calculators.

Takes
Plan a spot for a future date; scrub time and watch the light move.
Leaves
Opens onto a menu, not a map. Photographers consistently call it feature-rich and hard to use.
The Photographer's Ephemeris
iOS · Android · web · basic offline

The older, narrower tool that keeps its users: a map-centric sun and moon calculator showing how light falls on the land, opening straight onto the map with no menu in the way.

Takes
One screen, one job. Map on launch is the right default for a field tool.
Leaves
No sense of place quality — it will happily plan a beautiful sunrise over a parking lot.
GoldenHour.One
iOS · Android · freemium

Wraps weather around the ephemeris and scores it: a Sky Index for the chance of a dramatic sky and a Light Index for the quality of light, plus a bell that sets an early reminder.

Takes
Scoring conditions instead of listing them. A 0–10 sky number is a decision; a cloud percentage is homework.
Leaves
No locations. You bring your own spot.
Sun Surveyor · PhotoTime · Skylight
the long tail

Same ground, gentler curves: rise and set, twilights, shadow ratios, moon phase, AR compass overlays, widgets and golden-hour notifications. Skylight narrows to a single question — how good will tonight look.

Takes
Widgets and a pre-golden-hour notification are table stakes now.
Leaves
Nothing about crowds, access, or what other people are shooting.

Design language across all of them: dark map, dense numeric readouts, a time scrubber, coloured azimuth lines. Utility-grade, near-zero editorial voice, and no sense of what is worth shooting — you have to already know where you are going.

Two · Spot discovery
Wireframe · photo-grid discovery
search a city
tap a photo → coordinates, best time, fee, transit
Explorest
iOS · curated and crowdsourced

Built for the exact user you named: people who want the shot that performs. Spots come with GPS coordinates, hard-to-find access notes, transit instructions and weather.

Takes
Access notes as first-class content — how to reach it, not just where it is.
Leaves
Coverage is patchy outside its launch cities, and quality depends on submissions.
PhotoSpot · Photomapper
web and mobile maps

World maps of user-submitted shooting locations. Each pin carries a thumbnail, best time to visit, and entrance fee where relevant.

Takes
Best-time-to-visit stored per spot, not computed globally.
Leaves
Static. A spot is either in the database or not; nothing changes week to week.
Instagram Places and Snap Map
the default everyone already has

Tap a location tag or search a neighbourhood and see what is popular nearby, filtered by six broad categories.

Takes
Free, universal, and where your trend data ultimately comes from.
Leaves
Category filters are restaurants and cafés. There is no "waterfall at blue hour".

The structural weakness of this category is that a spot database goes stale and a photo grid has no sense of time. None of them can tell you that the colour peaks in six days or that the lot fills at nine.

Three · Save-from-a-reel
Wireframe · share-sheet import
reel shared from Instagram
↓ places extracted
Plotline · Stashed · JoySpot
iOS · 2025–26 · mostly free

A new category solving one thing well: share a reel to the app and it extracts every place mentioned and pins it to your map. Plotline reports over a million places mapped from social posts.

Takes
The share sheet as the primary input. This is how spots actually enter a creator’s life — from a reel, not a search.
Leaves
The map is a graveyard. Once pinned, nothing tells you when to go or whether it is still worth going.
Atlas Obscura
editorial, web and app

The counterweight to trend: unusual places largely ignored by Instagram, written up with a real voice.

Takes
Editorial authority. One well-written paragraph beats twelve data points.
Leaves
Deliberately not current, and not about photography.

Three rows are ours alone today: trend, season and route. Three are open circles. The recommendations below buy the two rows we are visibly missing — sky conditions and light direction — without buying the whole PhotoPills feature list, which is what made it hard to use.

What I recommend adding

Four new screens and three additions to screens that already exist. Ranked; I would build them in this order.

01
Conditions
New screen

A sky score for the next seven days at one spot, in the language of the app rather than a weather app: cloud cover shaped into a Sky Index, wind for the drone, mist probability at the falls, and a strip that names the best day of the week to go. Notifications ride on this — one push, forty-five minutes before the window, only when the score is worth the drive.

Why: This is the single most common feature across every light planner, and the only capability where we would look thin next to a free competitor.
02
Light direction
New screen

The sun-path overlay on one spot: which way the light arrives at golden hour, when the gorge wall stops shading the water, and how the frame changes across the day, with a scrubber. Compass-based AR is a later addition, not a launch requirement.

Why: The reason people pay for PhotoPills and Ephemeris. Without it, an experienced creator will keep a second app open.
03
Save from a reel
New screen

The share-sheet import: send a reel into The Scout, it identifies the location, matches it to a spot we already track, and offers it straight into a collection or today’s route with the light times already attached.

Why: This is the bridge nobody has built. The save-from-a-reel apps pin a dot; we would pin a dot that knows when to shoot it.
04
Before you go
New screen

One page of friction, per spot: permit or reservation status with a booking link, parking fill forecast by hour, gate times, fees, trail closures, cell coverage, washrooms, and how much of the walk is on rock. Dundas Peak is reservation-only; the Grotto parking is a four-hour commitment. That belongs on its own page, not in a footnote.

Why: Ontario-specific and genuinely hard to assemble. It is also the most defensible content moat in the product.
05
Go somewhere else
New screen

When a spot is peaking, crowded or reservation-locked, the app offers two alternates within thirty minutes that hold the same light and the same subject — the Hilton Falls move, made systematic.

Why: Turns the crowding problem the app creates into a feature, and gives casual users a reason to trust it over the trend itself.
06
Frame saturation
Addition to spot detail

Alongside the momentum figure, how many recent reels use the same composition — and which angles are underexposed. A rising spot shot from one bridge is a warning, not an invitation.

Why: The creators chasing views need the frame nobody has, not the place everybody has.
07
Weather re-plan and offline
Addition to the route

When the forecast turns, the route re-sorts and says so, with the rainy-day collection already loaded. The day’s route, light times and access notes cache for offline — most of these spots have no signal.

Why: The planners all treat offline as a feature. For an escarpment route it is a requirement.
And two things I would not build
Photography calculators

Depth of field, hyperfocal distance, exposure tables. This is what makes the incumbents feel like instruments. Reels are shot on phones; the calculator audience already owns PhotoPills.

A social feed with follows and comments

Instagram is the feed. Creator co-signs are a ranking signal on a location, not a timeline. Keep the profile as a body of work, not a place to post.

One risk worth naming

An app that ranks Ontario by reel volume accelerates exactly the crowding it reports. The Cheltenham Badlands were closed for years because of foot traffic on the shale. The design already hints at the answer — Hilton Falls surfaced as the quiet alternative to a booked-out Dundas Peak — and recommendation five makes it a rule rather than a coincidence. It is also the most defensible thing about the product: a trend app that knows when to send you somewhere else.
